# SWOT Analysis – Multiplayer RPG/Strategy Startup

**Who:** a group of 7 people from Warsaw planning to found a game studio. There is no company or funding yet, only a plan.

**Product idea (working title *Banners of Nav*):** an online multiplayer game that mixes RPG and strategy, set in Slavic dark fantasy (PEGI 16). Each player leads a hero who levels up over time and commands a small army. Players fight in 4v4 co-op/PvP matches that last about 40 minutes.

**Target niche:** "mid-core" players who want strategic depth and character progression but have no time for 3-hour strategy sessions or the toxicity of MOBAs.

---

## Strengths (S)

| # | Strength |
|---|---|
| S1 | The team covers the core roles (programming, game design, art, narrative), so it can build a prototype on its own |
| S2 | A distinctive setting: Slavic mythology instead of generic fantasy |
| S3 | A clear hybrid concept (RPG progression + strategy in short sessions) with few direct competitors |
| S4 | A small, flexible team that can change direction quickly based on player feedback |

## Opportunities (O)

| # | Opportunity | Exploited through |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Players are interested in games built on folklore and mythology (e.g. *The Witcher*, *Black Myth: Wukong*) | **S2**: a recognisable Slavic setting stands out in a crowded market |
| O2 | There is a gap between long, hardcore strategy games and MOBAs: many players want depth in short sessions | **S3**: our concept is aimed exactly at this gap |
| O3 | Early Access, Discord and streamers let a community grow alongside the game | **S4**: we can act quickly on player feedback and build a loyal core community |
| O4 | Poland's large gamedev scene: publishers, accelerators and support programmes for new studios | **S1**: a complete team with a playable prototype is attractive to publishers and accelerators |

---

## Weaknesses (W)

| # | Weakness |
|---|---|
| W1 | No company, no released game, no track record |
| W2 | No funding: the team works part-time, so progress is slow |
| W3 | No experience with online games (netcode, servers, balancing) |
| W4 | No marketing or community-building skills in the team |

## Threats (T)

| # | Threat | Amplified by |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Publishers and investors are cautious after the industry's wave of layoffs and prefer proven teams | **W1**: we have nothing to show that proves we can deliver |
| T2 | A competitor fills the niche first, or team members leave for paid jobs | **W2**: part-time work stretches development over years |
| T3 | A multiplayer game dies without enough players, and players leave quickly after a bad launch | **W3**: technical problems at launch (lag, empty matchmaking) drive the first players away |
| T4 | An oversaturated market where thousands of new games compete for attention every year | **W4**: without marketing, even a good game goes unnoticed |

---

## Conclusions

**S→O (use strengths to capture opportunities):**
- Build all branding and marketing around the Slavic mythology setting (S2→O1).
- Position the game clearly as "strategy depth in 40 minutes" (S3→O2).
- Run an open Discord and playtests from the first prototype (S4→O3).
- Apply to accelerators and publishers once there is a playable vertical slice (S1→O4).

**W→T (reduce weaknesses to avoid threats):**
- Release a small free demo or a game-jam version to build a track record (W1→T1).
- Narrow the scope to an MVP that a part-time team can actually finish (W2→T2).
- Start with co-op against AI, so the game is playable without a large player base, and add PvP later (W3→T3).
- Give one person the community role and build an audience before launch (W4→T4).
