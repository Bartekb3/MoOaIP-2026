# SWOT Analysis – CD PROJEKT RED

**Who:** a Polish AAA game studio from Warsaw, part of the CD PROJEKT group (listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange), which also owns the GOG.com store.

**Products:** *The Witcher* series (*The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt*) and *Cyberpunk 2077* with the *Phantom Liberty* expansion. In development: *The Witcher 4* and the next Cyberpunk game.

**Target niche:** players of large, story-driven single-player RPGs with open worlds and mature themes.

---

## Strengths (S)

| # | Strength |
|---|---|
| S1 | Two globally known brands: *The Witcher* and *Cyberpunk* |
| S2 | A reputation for strong storytelling, quests and characters |
| S3 | Large, generous expansions (*Blood and Wine*, *Phantom Liberty*) and long-term free updates |
| S4 | Own distribution channel (GOG) and a solid financial position from back-catalogue sales |
| S5 | Free remaster of *The Witcher 3* (December 2022) for PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X\|S, given to everyone who already owned the game. It brought back so many players that on Steam more people played at the same time than at the 2015 launch |

## Opportunities (O)

| # | Opportunity | Exploited through |
|---|---|---|
| O1 | Transmedia growth: Netflix *The Witcher*, *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners* bring new audiences to the games | **S1**: shows and anime act as free marketing for the brands |
| O2 | Steady demand for premium single-player RPGs, with no subscription or live-service fatigue | **S2**: story quality is exactly what this audience pays for |
| O3 | New platforms and generations (PS5, Xbox Series, Switch 2) let old games sell again | **S3**: updated, complete editions keep sales running for years |
| O4 | Moving to Unreal Engine 5 makes hiring easier and allows work on several projects at once | **S4**: stable finances fund the switch and parallel development |
| O5 | Physical trading card games are booming (*Pokémon*, *Magic: The Gathering*, *Disney Lorcana*, *One Piece*), so a Gwent TCG with collectible booster packs could sell well | **S1**: Gwent is already known and loved by *Witcher* fans, so a physical TCG starts with a ready audience |
| O6 | Card games with 3D visuals, where cards turn into animated units on a battlefield, look fresh next to flat 2D card games and are easier to show on streams and trailers | **S1**: a 3D Gwent can reuse well-known *Witcher* characters and monsters (Geralt, Ciri, Leshen) and the 3D models made for the main games, so fans recognise them instantly |

---

## Weaknesses (W)

| # | Weakness |
|---|---|
| W1 | Damaged trust after the buggy *Cyberpunk 2077* launch (2020), including its removal from PlayStation Store |
| W2 | Very long development cycles and few releases: revenue depends on 1–2 big games |
| W3 | History of crunch and overpromising in marketing |
| W4 | Loss of in-house engine know-how (REDengine) and staff turnover during the switch to UE5 |

## Threats (T)

| # | Threat | Amplified by |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Players and investors punish broken launches harshly (refunds, reviews, share price) | **W1**: another bad launch would hit an already fragile reputation |
| T2 | Rising AAA budgets and competition from other big RPGs (Bethesda, BioWare, Larian, Warhorse) | **W2**: one weak release would hurt the whole company |
| T3 | Competition for senior developers and stricter attention to working conditions | **W3**: crunch makes it harder to keep and attract talent |
| T4 | Delays or technical problems with the new engine and multiple parallel projects | **W4**: missing expertise slows development and raises risk |

---

## Conclusions

**S→O (use strengths to capture opportunities):**
- Time game marketing with new seasons of the Netflix show and *Edgerunners 2* (S1→O1).
- Stay focused on high-quality, story-driven single-player RPGs instead of chasing live-service trends (S2→O2).
- Keep releasing complete editions and ports of older games for new platforms (S3→O3).
- Use the UE5 switch to run several projects in parallel, funded by back-catalogue income (S4→O4).
- Partner with a card game publisher to release a physical Gwent TCG with booster packs (S1→O5).
- Make a new 3D Gwent, where played cards come to life as animated characters and monsters, reusing models from the main games (S1→O6).

**W→T (reduce weaknesses to avoid threats):**
- Release only when the game is stable on all platforms, and announce dates late (W1→T1).
- Shorten gaps between releases with smaller projects and expansions (W2→T2).
- Plan realistic schedules and avoid crunch to keep senior staff (W3→T3).
- Work closely with Epic Games and hire experienced Unreal developers (W4→T4).

---

## Strategy

**In short: grow, but don't rush.**
The company has more strengths than weaknesses, and people still want big story games. So it should make more games, but it can't afford another broken launch like *Cyberpunk 2077*.

| Area | What to do |
|---|---|
| Games | Keep making big story RPGs in *The Witcher* and *Cyberpunk* worlds. New worlds can wait |
| Making games | Build two or three games at the same time so fans don't wait 5+ years. Only release a game when it works well on every console |
| Promotion | Don't promise what the game can't deliver. Release games around the time new TV seasons or anime come out |
| Staff | Stop forcing long overtime, so experienced people stay |
| Money | Between big games, earn from expansions, new console versions of older games, and GOG |

**Main goal:** release a polished *The Witcher 4* to win back players' trust.

---

## Gwent (Gwint)

**What it is:** a card game that started as a mini-game in *The Witcher 3*. Many players loved it so much that CD PROJEKT RED made it a separate free-to-play game, *GWENT: The Witcher Card Game* (PC, consoles, mobile). It's free, and players can pay for extras like cosmetics and card packs.

**Where it's strong:** Gwent is one of the best-known mini-games in any RPG. Most *Witcher* fans know it. But the standalone game is small next to *Hearthstone*, and the studio has stopped making new content for it. The real strength is that **players know and like it**, not that the standalone game sells well.

| Idea | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Bring Gwent back in *The Witcher 4* | Fans expect it. It keeps players in the game for longer at a low cost |
| Link the mini-game to the mobile app | Unlock cards in the app by playing in *The Witcher 4*, and the other way round. This keeps players with the brand between big releases |
| Sell physical card decks | Collector's editions and stand-alone decks are cheap to make and fans buy them as merchandise |
| License Gwent to board game publishers | Earns money without using the studio's own developers |
| Use Gwent as an entry point to the brand | A free card game on phones is an easy way for new players to discover *The Witcher* before buying the big games |

**In short:** Gwent won't make as much money as a big RPG, but it's a cheap way to keep fans busy and make some money between releases. That helps with weakness W2 (long gaps between games).

### Gwent strategies

**1. Physical card game with a partner (O5, low risk)**
- Team up with an experienced card game publisher. CD PROJEKT RED gives the brand and art, the partner prints, sells and runs tournaments.
- Start small with a few starter decks. Add booster packs only if they sell well.
- The studio earns a share of every sale without moving its own developers off the big games.

**2. 3D Gwent as a side project (O6, medium risk)**
- A small separate team (or an outside studio) builds a 3D Gwent where cards come to life on the battlefield.
- Free to play, earning money from cosmetics only (card skins, boards, animations), not pay-to-win.
- Launch it in Early Access a few months before *The Witcher 4*, so it builds hype for the big game.

**3. One Gwent collection everywhere (O5 + O6, long term)**
- Link everything: playing Gwent in *The Witcher 4* unlocks cards in the 3D game, and physical booster packs include codes for digital cards.
- Fans get a reason to buy and play every Gwent product, not just one.
- Only worth doing once strategies 1 and 2 show that people actually buy and play them.
